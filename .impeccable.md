## Design Context

### Users

ZipQ is used primarily by real-estate brokers and agents who need to scan active transactions, deadlines, forms, signatures, clients, and follow-ups quickly during a busy workday. The interface should make the next required decision obvious without forcing users to decode implementation detail or repetitive status copy.

### Brand Personality

Calm, concise, operational. ZipQ should feel trustworthy and capable, with Sofia acting as a restrained assistant rather than a decorative character.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a light, quiet workspace with the low-contrast borders, neutral surfaces, compact controls, and progressive disclosure familiar from ChatGPT. Keep the layout airy enough to read at a glance while preserving the density needed for transaction work. Black is reserved for primary actions; semantic color is reserved for risk, warning, and success.

### Design Principles

1. Show brokers the decision, owner, and due state before supporting metadata.
2. Prefer one flat content surface with subtle dividers over nested cards and repeated borders.
3. Keep terminology stable across navigation, tables, forms, and detail views.
4. Use progressive disclosure for document fields and playbook work items; every disclosure must open, close, and expose its state to assistive technology.
5. Preserve readable rows while space is available; reflow only when the component's own width requires it.
6. Meet WCAG AA contrast, keyboard, focus, target-size, and zoom requirements without increasing visual noise.
